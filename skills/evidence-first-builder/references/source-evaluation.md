# Source and Candidate Evaluation

Use only the dimensions that can change the decision. Avoid fake precision and mechanical scoring.

## Evidence quality

Ask:
- Is this primary technical evidence, independent evidence, practitioner evidence, or promotion?
- Is it current enough for the claim?
- Can the claim be inspected or reproduced?
- Are version, environment, hardware, dataset, or test method disclosed?
- Are limitations and negative results visible?
- Is this source independent of the other sources supporting the claim?

## Repository health

Inspect when relevant:
- latest meaningful release/commit activity;
- issue and PR activity;
- maintainer responsiveness;
- supported platforms/runtime versions;
- tests, CI, docs, examples;
- license and redistribution terms;
- security policy/advisories;
- dependency quality and installation behavior.

Stars/downloads can help discover projects but do not prove fit or quality.

## Workflow/plugin health

Check:
- active maintenance;
- exact dependency availability;
- version pinning/migration notes;
- real reports on comparable hardware/software;
- memory/storage/runtime requirements;
- failure and fallback paths;
- license/model-use restrictions.

## App/service health

Check:
- current capabilities;
- current pricing and limits;
- export/interoperability and lock-in;
- privacy, telemetry, retention, and account requirements;
- platform support;
- independent reliability/support evidence;
- open-source/self-hosted alternatives.

## Incentive classification

Classify sources mentally as one or more of:
- primary technical evidence;
- independent evidence;
- community/practitioner evidence;
- vendor marketing;
- affiliate/comparison content;
- sponsored/promoted placement;
- marketplace listing.

The last four categories can surface candidates but do not independently validate quality.

## Commercial-bias warning signs

Downgrade confidence when evidence is dominated by:
- affiliate listicles;
- vague "best tool" pages without methodology;
- sponsored creator reviews that mirror vendor copy;
- vendor benchmarks without reproducible setup;
- SEO pages repeating one upstream claim;
- marketplace popularity without technical evidence;
- community praise lacking version/hardware/usage context.

## Candidate note

For meaningful choices, use working notes like:

`candidate | evidence | fit | maintenance | privacy/security | license/cost | lock-in | risks | verification | decision`

Do not expose the full note unless useful to the user.
